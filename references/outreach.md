# Openers

Only the top few leads get an opener. Draft one when their own words give you something to react to;
otherwise say so rather than inventing a hook.

## Shape

Two or three sentences. Reference what they wrote, connect it to the specific thing we solve, and ask one
easy question. No pitch, no feature list, no calendar link in the first message.

## Rules

- **Quote or paraphrase their actual words.** That is the whole reason this list is better than a bought
  one. If you cannot point at something they said, do not pretend to.
- **Name the specific problem they named**, not the category. "Handoffs between five tools" is theirs;
  "streamlining your GTM stack" is ours and means nothing to them.
- **One question, easy to answer.** "How are you handling X today?" beats "Do you have 15 minutes?"
- **No flattery.** "Love your post!" reads as a template, because it is one.
- **Never claim to have met them**, been referred, or used their product.
- **Say where you saw them** — that they commented on a specific post. It is true, it explains the
  message, and it is the least creepy option available.

## Worked example

> **Cynthia De Kock — CEO, RevReady.** Commented on a post about qualification routing:
> *"Qualified answers showing the booking page instantly while unqualified submissions trigger a Slack
> alert is such a clean way to handle it."*

> Cynthia — saw your comment on Fivos' post about routing qualified answers straight to a booking page.
> The part that usually breaks for teams doing that at volume is the email step before it: the sequence
> lands in spam and the routing never gets a chance to fire. How are you handling sending reputation
> today?

Specific, references her actual words, names a problem adjacent to the one she raised, ends with one
answerable question.

## When to skip

- Reaction-only leads. Nothing to react to. Rank them, do not write to them.
- Generic praise. "Great thread" gives you nothing.
- Anyone scored `competitor` or `employee`.
